Model Editor V21 - what changed from V20, and the proof
======================================================

THE ASK (2026-11-16)
--------------------
The redrawn man's 3D GLB was loaded in V20 and ruled
"not acceptable as is": the completion discs around
the joints (radius 486-534 px from the stage-2 circle
redraw) are so large the assembled man reads as one
blob. The fix: make the points' radius adjustable so
the areas drawn to fill in can be made to look right
manually - a slider with the radius for each point,
the slider changing the circle's size, and the point
placement changing the centre of the circle that fills
in the areas. Refinement: all of the circles should
not be the same size either - the wrists and the
ankles would be some of the smallest, until fingers
are added. Direction: the work is making it 3D first
and then back to 2D paper.
Then the fill-source ruling: where 3D sweep data
exists, the fill is NOT a circle at all - it is a
per-joint REVEAL BITMAP, the model's own rendered
pixels from 5-degree pose sweeps, composited; the
slider is the manual fallback where no bitmap exists.
And the standing product contract: custom tools built
in; the Ai pipeline fully automated first; each step
adjustable afterwards, re-running from that step; a
modern user-friendly format (text fully visible, no
scrollbars or slide bars hiding it, panels resizable);
and all features requested carried in every version.
And the four-view standard (2026-11-16, on the
SandBase multi-view benchmark's measured view-count
economics - each added true view buys down error on
the faces it reveals, and the second side is the one
the doll set currently derives): the input set is
FOUR pictures - front, back, left side, right side.

1. THE PER-POINT FILL RADIUS (the editor)
-----------------------------------------
- Selecting a joint point shows a Fill radius row in
  the Selected panel (slider id fRadius, typed number
  box fRadiusNum, readout fRadiusVal), mirroring the
  Angle row. The slider's range is centred on THAT
  point's radius (20% to 180% of it), so a wrist's
  slider tops out far below a hip's.
- The fill circle is drawn as an orange outline over
  the part (div class fillCircle), centred on the
  point's pin and sized to its radius. Dragging the
  point - or typing in the Pin X/Y fields - moves the
  circle's centre with the point (movePin refreshes
  it), because the pin is the circle's centre.
- Each point keeps its own radius. Parts that share
  one joint (a split carrier sits on its joint's pin
  beside the joint's own child part) share the joint's
  radius: they are the same point on screen, and the
  completion keeps one radius per joint
  (ME21_applyRadius).
- A point with no radius set shows the completion's
  computed default - (base radius + 300) x 1.42 + 4,
  the redraw engine's own Disc_Radius - marked
  "(auto)", and stays unset until its slider moves.

2. ANATOMICAL SEEDING (Seed_Disc_Radii_V1.PY)
---------------------------------------------
The man's redrawn rig no longer starts from the
near-uniform 486-534 discs. Each joint's seeded radius
is measured from the joint's own local part width in
the artwork: the cross-section width of the parent
part at the pin, on the line through the pin
perpendicular to the bone axis, measured on the
pre-completion split parts (alpha runs bridged across
gaps up to 160 px; the widest cross-section within
160 px along the axis, because pins sit at part ends).
Seed = round(1.42 x width/2) + 4, minimum 12. Nothing
is hard-coded per joint name - fingers, when they
come, will simply measure smaller.
Seeded radii (disc_radius in the rig and GLBs):
  Front:     waist 261, spine 209, neck 169,
             shoulder 184/174, elbow 130/138,
             wrist 92/92, hip 286/286, knee 122/117,
             ankle 105/103
  Back:      waist 261, spine 201, neck 161,
             shoulder 161/161, elbow 145/145,
             wrist 91/91, hip 219/220, knee 136/136,
             ankle 98/98
  SideRight and SideLeft (identical):
             waist 235, spine 173, neck 77,
             shoulder 248/204, elbow 151/118,
             wrist 90/77, hip 191/284, knee 199/148,
             ankle 95/91
Wrists (77-92) and ankles (91-105) are the smallest,
elbows and knees mid, hips, shoulders, neck, waist
and spine largest - the specified ordering, out of
the measurements. (The side hip_f 284 reflects the
known wide side shorts band; the side neck 77 is the
neck column's true front-to-back width in profile.)

3. PERSISTENCE - WHERE THE RADII LIVE
-------------------------------------
- The rig JSON: every joint of
  Paper_Doll_Redraw_V2/Redrawn_Rig_V1.JSON carries
  disc_radius (and, after a completion run,
  fill_source).
- The GLBs: Build_Redrawn_GLB_V2.PY stamps
  extras.disc_radius on each joint's child node and
  on split carriers, and the top-level extras.joints
  table carries disc_radius per joint. Verified by
  read-back: 68 of 77 nodes carry it in both the flat
  and 3D GLBs (the 9 without are face features and
  roots that carry no joint).
- The editor: GLB open reads extras.disc_radius
  (with the joints table as fallback, matched by
  pin - which also covers the Pelvis root, whose pin
  is the waist pin); Save GLB writes it back; the
  Save rig points file carries radius per point;
  undo snapshots carry it.

4. THE COMPLETION USES THEM (Editor_Pipeline_V1)
------------------------------------------------
The editor's Ai Redraw step sends the tuned radii as
disc_radii (view -> joint -> radius); the pipeline's
Step_Redraw writes them to Disc_Radii_Overrides_V1.JSON
and hands the engine REDRAW_DISC_RADII_JSON. The
engine's Disc_Radius_For decides per joint: per-run
override, then the rig's own disc_radius field, then
the computed default. A point with no set radius
falls back to the computed radius.

5. THE REVEAL BITMAP - THE DATA PATH WINS
-----------------------------------------
Per joint, the fill source precedence is now:
  (1) the reveal bitmap, where one exists;
  (2) the disc at the per-point radius (manual path).
The bitmap contract: a full-canvas RGBA PNG per view
and joint at <REDRAW_REVEAL_DIR>/<View>/
Reveal_<joint name>_V1.PNG - the 3D lane's composite
of the model's own rendered pixels from 5-degree pose
sweeps (averaged where steps overlap, by the
producer). Where a bitmap exists the engine fills
from the bitmap's own shape AND colours on both sides
of the joint and draws NO disc for that joint at all;
bitmap pixels are never outline-repainted (their
colours are the model's own, edge included). The
pipeline's Step_Redraw takes Params["reveal_dir"]
and passes REDRAW_REVEAL_DIR; the editor's Redraw
step sends the Reveal bitmaps folder from Settings.
The written rig and census record each joint's
fill_source ("reveal_bitmap" or "disc").

6. THE STANDING PRODUCT CONTRACT
--------------------------------
1. Custom tools built in: the Joint Placer paint
   panel, the per-point radius slider, typed angle
   entry, the Ai menu, and the machine-path Settings
   all stay in the product. Nothing moved out.
2. Full automation first: "Run the whole pipeline"
   runs Make the 3D model -> Snapshots -> Slice ->
   Redraw -> Rig it -> Make it paper in the 3D-first
   order, each step feeding the next from the page
   session, no hand steps. (Make it 3D stays one
   click away, as before.)
3. Every step adjustable after: the step list shows
   each chain step with its state (not run yet /
   running / done / failed), its adjustment note, and
   a "Re-run from here" button that re-runs that step
   and every step after it - never the steps before.
   Steps with nothing adjustable (Snapshots, Make it
   paper) say so plainly in their row.
4. Modern format: the Ai log grows with its content
   and wraps (no max-height, no scrollbar cap); the
   step panel and the save box resize by their
   corners; the V20 resizable left panel carries
   forward.
5. All features requested: the build census carries
   every V20 feature marker (135, names advanced)
   plus the V21 markers - 162/162 in the built file.

7. DIRECTION - 3D FIRST, THEN PAPER
-----------------------------------
The Ai flow presents and runs the 3D-first order: the
Ai hint states it, Make the 3D model offers Snapshots,
the automated chain runs 3D -> snapshots -> slice ->
redraw -> rig -> paper, and the paper is derived from
the 3D (snapshots and sliced layers of the model).

8. THE FOUR-VIEW INPUT SET
--------------------------
The Ai flow presents four pictures as the standard
input (the pick is labelled "front, back, left side,
right side, any order"), and the pipeline's
detect_views names all four from the unordered set:
the sides are the stride profiles (widest silhouettes),
front vs back is decided in the head region, and the
two sides are told apart by the facing score - the
feet band's centroid toward the facing direction plus
the head band's mass skew, both flipping sign under
mirroring. The sign convention is calibrated on the
man's set: his source side picture measures +0.0568
and is the rig's SideRight view; its mirror measures
-0.0568 and names side_left. (The local image-to-3D
lane is unchanged - it builds from the front; the
editor's in-page paper doll builder keeps its own
three-picture pick and derives the second side itself,
as it always has.) A legacy three-picture set still
classifies, its single side named by its facing and
carried under the old "side" key. The 3D tool still
receives the detected front picture, as before.

BIT 2 - TORRENT SECTIONS (elements 4 and 11; issue 46)
------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-11-17, Bit 2 of the Version 22 Assembly
Plan. The Sections panel stands in the panel after
the Detail section, in the page's own section shape:
a Sections count (sectionCount), an operation picker
(torrentOperation: wave, scale, rotate, translate -
the torrent module's own operations), a Run button
(runSections) and the stitch line (stitchStatus),
inside sectionsSection.

Heavy operations route through the torrent sections
route: the pipeline's torrent step (Step_Torrent)
runs Torrent_Sections_V1.PY on the open model's GLB -
the operation runs section by section, then the
stitch pass runs over the whole model. The panel's
stitch line shows the verdict from the step report:
seam mismatches counted, the watertight verdict, and
the triangle count against the slider target, with
the whole-model compare (the sectioned run equals
the whole run; the step runs with --verify-whole).
An operation is not reported done until the stitch
pass says so: the editor reloads a result only when
the step report's stitch pass passed, and on a
stitch failure the line names the seam mismatches
and the open model is unchanged. The detail step
routes through this same route: after the detail
module rewrites the geometry, its result runs
through the torrent program (the identity operation,
scale factor 1.0, section by section with the stitch
pass and the whole-model compare) and the torrent
output is what the editor reloads; a stitch failure
fails the detail step. Sculpt arrives in Bit 3 and
routes through this route when the model is large,
as the plan states. The rig and paper steps'
products are layered multi-node GLBs, outside the
torrent module's single-solid-mesh scope, and are
not flattened through it.

The plugin judging half of the final pass - a plugin
judging the stitch report plus rendered views -
wires in Bit 7. Until then the deterministic stitch
numbers alone gate the operation. That is stated
plainly here, as the plan requires: no plugin
judgement is claimed for a Bit 2 operation.

Scope, declared in the panel too: the torrent step
carries geometry and COLOR_0 only; it does not carry
skinning, animation or textures. A model with a
skins array or JOINTS_0 / WEIGHTS_0 attributes is
refused with a plain log line and the reason on the
stitch line - never silently stripped.
